# Professional reader voice audition

**Status:** Script only. No voice, provider, recording rights or spend approved. The sample is fictional and is not part of the published edition. Target: 45–60 seconds at a composed 150–165 words per minute, with natural pauses and no music.

## Read aloud

At a neighborhood market, Maya notices a duplicate $24.80 authorization after tapping to pay. The first screen says “pending,” but never tells her whether money has moved. A 3.5 percent processing fee appears on the receipt, too. Authorization and settlement are different steps; a clear receipt should name the payment method, show the final amount, and explain when a hold should disappear.

Now to banking. A small bank’s API reports 99.9 percent uptime, yet its fictional outage drill leaves customers without an answer for eighteen minutes. The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, or FDIC, does not resolve an app outage. The bank improves its recovery plan by measuring both system restoration and the time it takes someone to reach useful help.

Across these stories, the question is simple: can people see what happened, correct an error, and find help when the usual path fails?

## Pronunciation and acceptance

- “Maya” as **MY-uh**; “API” as **A-P-I**; “FDIC” as **F-D-I-C**.
- Read “$24.80” as “twenty-four dollars and eighty cents,” “3.5 percent” as “three point five percent,” and “99.9 percent” as “ninety-nine point nine percent.”
- Leave a short section pause before “Now to banking.” Avoid a dramatic newsreader cadence.
- Compare the approved recording against this exact text. Record the voice/provider, usage rights, script revision, duration and checksum before attaching audio to an edition.
